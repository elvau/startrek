Privacy policy

Translation for convenience. The German version (Datenschutzerklärung) is legally binding.

1. Controller

Daniel Jacek Klein, Cecilienstraße 31, 41236 Mönchengladbach, Germany · Email: danielklein@splitandfly.com

2. In short

  • The site is delivered via Google Firebase Hosting.
  • Without an account, your trips stay only in your browser. I cannot see them.
  • With an account, trips are stored in Google Firebase so you can use them on several devices and share them with others.
  • For flight and accommodation searches, only places, dates and the number of people are sent to the providers, no names and no email addresses.
  • Booking details (ID card, passport) are optional, are stored only in your account and go nowhere else.
  • The AI planner (beta) is only available with an account; your travel request is sent to Google Gemini for it.
  • The maps (accommodation search, “Trip map”) load map images from OpenFreeMap only when you open them; the small route images (start page, day plan) load a map section as soon as a trip has a route.
  • Bug reports (beta) are stored in a non-public project on GitHub; images are deleted after 30 days.
  • There are no advertising trackers, no analytics tools and no cookies from me. Fonts are served from this site.

4. Storage in your browser

The app stores trips, saved people and groups, settings (e.g. language, currency, light or dark mode, sorting of the start page), the ECB daily exchange rates for conversion, when you last changed a trip (for “Last edited”) and recently used search options in your browser’s storage (localStorage) so they are still there on your next visit. With an account, Firebase additionally stores your sign-in and a copy of your trips in the browser (IndexedDB) so the app also works offline; when signing in with an email link, the app remembers the email address until you open the link. Booking details are not stored in the browser (see section 5). All of this is strictly necessary for the function you request (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). You can delete this data at any time in your browser settings.

5. Account and sharing (Google Firebase)

If you create an account or share a trip, you use Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore by Google (Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland; parent company Google LLC, USA). The following is processed:

  • for signing in: email address or Google account (name, email, profile picture), time of sign-in, IP address;
  • for saved trips: the trip data you enter (e.g. names and ages of fellow travellers, on request their nationality for entry information, postcode and town of residence, amounts, in the kitty expenses during the trip and which family paid what, for shared trips also who submitted, confirmed or disputed an expense, with account name and reason), as well as who may view or edit a trip;
  • for saved people and groups: the details below (readable only by you).

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the function you request). If you enter fellow travellers, you should let them know. The data is kept until you delete the trip or your account; write to me if you would like help with this. Google is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. More: Firebase and privacy.

Saved people and booking details

Under “Groups and people” you can enter a name, date of birth or age and an approximate place of residence (postcode) for each saved person. The app uses this to calculate the age at the start of the trip and the journey to the airport. Trips only receive the age and the family’s place of residence, not the date of birth. Without an account this stays in your browser; with an account it is also stored in Firestore, readable only by you.

You can also save preferences, for yourself and per group: preferred airports, the postcode of your home (only in your own preferences and only with an account, never stored in the browser as part of the preferences; if you apply it in the flight search at the push of a button, it becomes part of the trip as place of residence, is stored like the trip and is visible to fellow travellers; it is not sent to the AI planner), stops, luggage, accommodation type, travel style, budget, travel periods, a short note and countries you do not want to travel to or fly over. Like the people, they are stored in the browser or in your account and pre-fill searches and the AI planner. Blocked countries say nothing about the reason; please do not put personal or sensitive information in the note.

Optionally you can save booking details (names as on the ID, gender, nationality, ID card and passport number with validity, issuing country) to make later bookings quicker. These details:

  • are only available with an account; they are stored separately from everything else in Firestore and only you can read them, not even fellow travellers or invited people;
  • are not stored in the browser (neither in localStorage nor in the cache) but only loaded when you open them;
  • can, on request, be used under “Important” to check whether a passport is valid long enough for the trip; this check runs only in your browser, the expiry date does not end up in the trip and is not visible to fellow travellers;
  • are not sent into trips, to the search service, to the providers, to the AI planner or into bug reports;
  • can be deleted at any time per person or completely (“Delete all booking details”); deleting a person deletes them too.

Technically the data is stored at Google (see above) and is in principle accessible to me as the operator of the Firebase project. I do not look at it and use it only to show it to you. Nothing is currently booked through Split&Fly; the data will only be used once there is a booking function and you trigger a booking yourself. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) and (b) GDPR (voluntary information for the function you request). Only enter other people’s data with their consent.

6. Search service for flights and accommodation (Cloudflare)

When you search for flights or accommodation, the app sends the request to my search service at Cloudflare (Cloudflare Inc., 101 Townsend St., San Francisco, CA 94107, USA). Cloudflare technically sees your IP address. The search service does not store requests and then queries the providers. It only counts anonymously how often which function and which provider is used (without account, IP address or search content) so I can keep an eye on the free quotas. To prevent abuse it also counts how many requests (searches, AI planner, bug reports, clicks) come from one IP address; this counter is kept only in Cloudflare’s cache and expires after one day at the latest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, legitimate interest in protecting the service). Only the following is sent to the providers: departure and destination places, travel dates, number of adults/children/infants (for accommodation the children’s ages) and search options (e.g. stops, luggage). Providers do not receive names, email addresses or your IP address.

If you have not yet entered a place of residence (postcode), the flight search asks the search service for your approximate location in order to suggest nearby departure airports. Cloudflare estimates it from your IP address (country, town, coordinates rounded to about 10 km). The search service only returns it to your app and stores nothing; the app keeps it in memory only until the page is closed (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, legitimate interest in suitable suggestions). For the journey to the airport the app still uses only the postcode you enter yourself.

7. AI planner (Google Gemini, beta)

If you use the AI planner, the app sends your travel request (the text you enter), the number and ages of the travellers, the airports near your home, your preferences (without names, see section 5) and – if already entered – destination and travel dates to my search service. It checks your sign-in, counts requests per day (user ID and date, deleted after two days) and passes the request and search results on to Google Gemini (Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland). Google does not receive names, email addresses or your IP address. Please do not write personal data into the travel request.

If you ask the AI assistant within an open trip, a summary of that trip is also sent: destination, travel dates and the items (area, name, status, amount, for flights route and times). Names of travellers (also in names such as “Flight family …”), notes, booking numbers, payments, kitty expenses and booking details (ID card, passport) are not sent. The app only applies changes made by the AI once you confirm them.

Important: During the beta I use the free tier of the Gemini API. Under the Gemini API terms of service, Google may use the transmitted content (travel request and search results) to provide and improve its services; people at Google may also read it. Therefore do not enter names, contact details or other personal or confidential information in the travel request. If I switch to a paid tier, this use no longer applies and I will update this section.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (you trigger the request). Use is voluntary.

8. Connecting AI assistants (MCP, beta)

In the account menu you can create a personal key with which your own AI assistant (e.g. Claude, ChatGPT or a program like Cursor) can search via Split&Fly and create and extend trips in your account. The key contains your account ID, a key ID, your display name and the date of issue and is signed with a secret key of the search service; it is not stored anywhere. Blocked key IDs are listed in the search service’s settings.

When you use the key, your AI assistant sends requests to my search service. It searches as described in section 6, keeps offers found for a few hours in Cloudflare’s cache (so the assistant can add them to a trip) and counts the searches per key and day (key ID and date, deleted after two days). For trips in your account the search service reads and writes your trips in Firebase (section 5), only those you are a member of, and changes them only with edit rights. The assistant receives destination, travel dates, number of travellers by age group and the items with prices, no names of travellers and no booking details (ID card, passport).

What you enter into your assistant and what it retrieves from here is processed by its provider (e.g. Anthropic, OpenAI) as your own provider under its terms; I have no influence on this. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (you create the key and trigger the requests). Use is voluntary.

9. Reporting bugs (beta)

With the red “🐞 Report a bug” button you can report bugs when signed in. The following is sent to my search service at Cloudflare: your description, an image if you attach one, as well as the page opened (without invitation code), language, browser and device, screen size, app version, the latest error messages in the browser, the kind of view (e.g. “trip, 3 people”, without names or trip data) and the latest trip switches in the app (time, reason such as “newly created” or “start page” and the random ID of the trip, without names or contents). The search service checks your sign-in and counts reports per day (user ID and date, deleted after two days).

The report is stored as an entry in a non-public project on GitHub, together with a shortened user ID so I can match the account if I have questions (GitHub Inc., 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA; transfer under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework). Images are stored in Cloudflare’s storage (R2) and deleted automatically after 30 days; I delete reports once the bug is fixed. Please make sure screenshots do not show other people’s data you do not want to share. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) and (f) GDPR (you send the report voluntarily; my legitimate interest is a bug-free app).

10. Links to providers and partner links

Links such as “At the provider ↗”, Booking.com, Airbnb, Google Flights, Skyscanner, GetYourGuide, Viator, Tiqets, Google Maps or Tripadvisor open the provider’s page. Only then does that provider receive data from you (e.g. IP address), and its privacy policy applies. Links marked “Partner*” in the app contain my partner ID so the provider can attribute a booking to me; for this the provider or the partner network (Travelpayouts, Viator) may set cookies on its own site. I do not learn who booked, only that and for what a commission is due.

When you tap a link to a provider, the app only tells my search service which provider and which kind of link it was (e.g. “Viator, activities”). The search service counts this per day to see which providers are used. Neither IP address nor account, trip or search details are stored.

11. Map (OpenFreeMap)

When you tap “Map” in the accommodation search, “Trip map” or “Map, image & animation” in the day plan, your browser loads map images and fonts for the map from OpenFreeMap (openfreemap.org), a free map service with data from OpenStreetMap. OpenFreeMap receives technically necessary data such as your IP address, the time and the map section shown (i.e. roughly the place you are looking at). Names, trips or prices are not sent there; the app places accommodation, events and airports on the map itself. OpenFreeMap sets no cookies. In addition, the small route images on the start page and in the day plan show a map section: for this the browser loads the map images of that section once from OpenFreeMap and keeps the finished image locally. Otherwise nothing is loaded without opening a map. Image and video of the trip route are created only in your browser; they are not uploaded anywhere, you share or save them yourself. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in showing you where accommodation is located). The “Google Maps ↗” link only opens Google Maps when tapped (see section 10).

12. Campaign page for contributions

Signed-in users can publish a campaign page for a trip through which e.g. family, sponsors or a club fund can chip in. The page can be read by anyone who knows the link, even without an account. Only the following is public there: title and text of the campaign, place and dates of the trip, goal and progress (totals of the contributions entered) as well as a PayPal.me name and/or the link to a fundraiser at a well-known provider (GoFundMe, Leetchi, betterplace.me, Ko-fi, Revolut). Account holder and IBAN are not collected. Names of fellow travellers and individual contributions do not appear. It is only published with the explicit consent of the person creating the page (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR); it is stored in Cloud Firestore (see section 5) and can be deleted at any time via “Withdraw”, after which the link no longer works. Please only enter your own PayPal name or fundraiser, or one with the owner’s consent.

Money never flows through Split&Fly: payments via PayPal or the fundraiser go directly to whoever set them up. Visitors of the page are not tracked; the page loads its content without an account and without cookies. Tapping the PayPal.me link or the fundraiser link takes you to that provider, whose privacy notices apply.
